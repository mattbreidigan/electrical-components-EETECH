§01

The brief, read back

The posting asks for accurate models and photoreal renders of electrical devices — chips and semiconductors, converters, memory and storage media, connectors — built to hold up under close-up camera moves and to animate cleanly, working from technical drawings and datasheets.

Storage media is one of those categories, so this piece takes a single representative part all the way: a datasheet-accurate model, a materials pass, an engineering-style dimension drawing, and the turntable / exploded / macro shots the posting calls out — end to end, the way a real delivery would run.

One model covers three of the four categories at once: the storage device itself, the semiconductors on its board (controller, DRAM, V-NAND — each a modeled BGA package, not a decal), and the M-key edge connector.

§03

Holds up close

The label is recreated vector artwork on a near-Lambertian laminate; the case detail is real geometry. Toggle the wireframe to see the topology under the beauty pass — bevel-weighted hard-surface, no n-gons, no overlapping shells, custom normals on every chamfer.

§04

Built to animate

Hard-surface, so "animate cleanly" means clean pivots and a clean hierarchy rather than deformation: every part is a separately named object, origins set where they'd rotate or pull, the whole thing parented under one root. A layer index on each part drives the exploded move — label, heat-spreader film, gap-fill, each chip, the PCB, the M2 screw — as one Z-offset per tier, and reverses to reassemble.

§05

Materials & labels

PBR throughout. Black solder-mask PCB with ENIG-gold pads and white silkscreen; nickel lid on the controller; black epoxy on the NAND and DRAM with laser-etched top marks; the heat-spreader label is matte laminate with a copper film underside, exactly as the datasheet describes it.

All printed artwork is recreated from scratch — vector label, silkscreen, and chip marks drawn programmatically. No manufacturer photography is used anywhere in the build.
